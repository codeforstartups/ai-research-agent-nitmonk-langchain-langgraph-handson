# Internal Note: AI Coding-Agent Market Overview (illustrative)

> Sample/synthetic data for the course. Numbers are illustrative, not real.

## Segment definition

We define "AI coding agents" as tools that go beyond autocomplete to take
multi-step software engineering actions: reading a repository, editing multiple
files, running tests, and iterating toward a goal. This is distinct from
inline-completion assistants, though the market boundary is blurry.

## Rough segmentation

- **Inline assistants** — autocomplete and chat inside the editor. Highest
  adoption, lowest autonomy.
- **Agentic IDEs** — editors with a built-in planning/apply loop over the whole
  workspace.
- **Autonomous agents** — asynchronous "give it a ticket, get a PR" systems.
  Highest ambition, least mature reliability.

## Illustrative demand signals

- Developer willingness-to-pay is concentrated in productivity gains on
  boilerplate, test generation, and migration work.
- Enterprise buyers gate on security, IP indemnification, and audit trails far
  more than individual developers do.
- Reliability (does the change actually work?) is the dominant differentiator
  once novelty wears off.

## Takeaway

The market is expanding but crowded at the inline-assistant layer. Differentiated
value is increasingly at the **agentic/autonomous** layer, where reliability and
enterprise trust are unsolved.
